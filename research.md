## The embodiment record (T1)

- **Botvinick and Cohen (1998)**, Nature 391: 756: the rubber-hand illusion; ownership follows synchronous visuotactile evidence.
- **Samad, Chung and Shams (2015)**, PLOS ONE 10: e0117178: ownership as Bayesian causal inference over whether visual and tactile signals share a bodily cause; the model this paper extends with a morphology prior.
- **Blanke, Slater and Serino (2015)**, Neuron 88: 145–166: bodily self-consciousness decomposed into ownership, self-location, and first-person perspective; the factorization precedent.
- **Seth (2013)**, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 17: 565–573: interoceptive inference and the embodied self.
- **Guterstam, Petkova and Ehrsson (2011)**, PLOS ONE 6: e17208: the illusion of owning a third arm; supernumerary ownership.
- **Arai, Saito, Fukuoka, Ueda et al. (2022)**, Scientific Reports 12: embodiment of supernumerary robotic limbs in virtual reality, with peripersonal-space correlates rather than questionnaires alone.
- **Won, Bailenson, Lee and Lanier (2015)**, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication 20: 241–259 (10.1111/jcc4.12107): homuncular flexibility; participants learn to control avatars with nonhuman morphologies, including three-armed bodies.
- **Krekhov, Cmentowski and Krüger (2019)**, arXiv:1907.05220, in Proceedings of the Annual Symposium on Computer-Human Interaction in Play (10.1145/3311350.3347172): the illusion of animal body ownership; spiders and bats.
- **Khan, Trinh, Lisle and Do (2026)**, arXiv:2606.26364, June 24, 2026: "Having Dog Ears 'for Real'"; active and passive haptics increase the sense of embodiment for nonhuman body parts, and the paper's own framing notes that embodying nonhuman parts is a practice of specific subcultures.
- **Yamamura, Kondo, Sakurada and Normand (2026)**, Frontiers in Virtual Reality 7 (10.3389/frvir.2026.1817800): the imaginary cat ears illusion; congruent multisensory manipulation raised ownership of virtual cat ears with no corresponding proprioceptive drift. This dissociation is the paper's argument for a factorized phenotype.
- **Yee and Bailenson (2007)**, Human Communication Research 33: 271–290: the Proteus effect.
- **Mottelson, Muresan, Hornbæk and Makransky (2023)**, ACM TOCHI 30: 1–42 (10.1145/3590767): systematic review and meta-analysis of body-ownership illusions across 111 articles; modest ownership effects, stronger agency effects, and pervasive weaknesses in power and terminology.

## The community record (T1)

- **Kranjec et al. (2019)**, "Illusory Body Perception and Experience in Furries," Proceedings of the Cognitive Science Society (eScholarship 37x5162v): 50 furries compared against previously collected non-furry data; furries reported **weaker** subjective embodiment of a human rubber hand, and within the furry sample, lower valuation of the human form predicted less proprioceptive drift toward the human hand. The result inverts the naive global-plasticity prediction and is the empirical hinge of this paper.
- **Clegg, Collings and Roxburgh (2019)**, Society and Animals 27: 403–426 (10.1163/15685306-12341540): therianthropy, wellbeing, schizotypy, and autism; 112 therians against 265 comparison participants, with unusual experiences, introvertive anhedonia, and autism-quotient scores predicting therian status, and the odds of exceeding the autism-quotient cutoff about 5.9 times higher among therians with a wide interval, 2.04 to 17.31. Cross-sectional; causality unresolved.
- **Grivell, Clegg and Roxburgh (2014)**, Identity 14: 113–135: interpretative phenomenological analysis of therian identity from 5 in-depth interviews; phantom body parts and mental shifts as recurring themes.
- **Luiggi-Hernández, Fein, Bradley and Pelly (2025)**, The Humanistic Psychologist 53: 40–58 (10.1037/hum0000350): empirical phenomenological study of embodied alterhuman experience; the experiences involve body, perception, environment, affect, and behavior rather than belief alone.
- **Roberts, Plante, Gerbasi and Reysen (2015)**, Anthrozoös 28: 533–548: the anthrozoomorphic identity; factor analysis separating liking an animal, spiritual connection, and identification as that species, so "I like wolves" and "wolf is me" are psychometrically distinct.
- **Palmer-Cooper, McGuire and Wright (2021)**, Cognitive Neuropsychiatry 27: 86–104: unusual experiences and metacognition across tulpamancy, ASMR, and control groups, 243 participants, excluding diagnosed psychotic disorders; unusual sensory experience without a general elevation in delusion-proneness.
- **Taylor, Hodges and Kohányi (2003)**, Imagination, Cognition and Personality 22: 361–380: the illusion of independent agency in adult fiction writers; experienced character autonomy is not confined to any one community.
- **Blom and Sharpless (2025)**, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews 174: 106193: systematic review of clinical therianthropy proposing a spectrum conceptualization, with the authors' own caution against equating nonclinical identities with clinical presentations. Cited strictly as a boundary condition.

## The natural-experiment record (T1)

- **Curran, Newschaffer, Lee, Crawford et al. (2007)**, Pediatrics 120: e1386–e1392: behaviors associated with fever in children with autism spectrum disorders; 30 febrile and 30 afebrile matched children, transient reductions on several aberrant-behavior subscales.
- **Byrne et al. (2022)**, medRxiv 2022.05.23.22275374: prospective monitoring of 141 autistic children and 103 comparison children over 3 months; of 50 autistic children who had a fever, 3 showed reported behavioral improvement, while the typical pattern was worsening. The paper carries both studies together, since the pair is the honest picture of a natural experiment that did not replicate at scale.

## Facts the simulation establishes (properties of stated models, never of people)

- Design discrimination: under the designs the literature has run (a single human effector, one morphology), global plasticity, morphology-congruent binding, and expectancy account for the data nearly equally, with model-recovery accuracy close to chance among the three; under a crossed design manipulating morphology and identity congruence within participants, recovery rises sharply, and the required sample per group is computed from the interaction effect rather than from a main effect.
- The one-knob-two-signs result: a Bayesian causal-inference model of ownership with a morphology-congruence prior reproduces both the observed weaker human-hand ownership among nonhuman-identifying participants and stronger congruent-nonhuman ownership, from a single shifted parameter, so the field's anomalous finding is a prediction of the framework rather than a problem for it.
- The dissociation: when ownership and proprioceptive drift read different latents of the same inference, congruent evidence moves ownership without moving drift, reproducing the cat-ear pattern qualitatively as a model prediction.
- The multiscale reachable set: fast state and slow model parameters produce hysteresis, with a measurable fraction of a trained configuration persisting after perturbation ends; SELF and OTHER learning rates are separably estimable; and the detectability of the proposed double dissociation is computed as a function of the learning-rate gap and sample size.
